# Code Quest Lab — feel: full screen, bigger tools, hints, gravity, depth

**Status:** Approved by Papa, 2026-10-04. In chat, from the pasted "Lab of Curiosity: Full-Screen, Interactive Hints, Gravity Ingredients, Larger Elements" brief, Papa chose **Wider room** (D1), **1.5× + re-layout** (D2), **Bobbing arrows** (D4) and **Falls, then home** (D5). Papa then answered "yes go" to the summary of D1–D5 and in the same message asked for two more things: "show the name of the action clearly below the interactable item" (→ D3) and "make the overall like 3D environment … like the planet, the planet feel 3D" (→ D6). D3 and D6 are written from that request; Papa approved them on review of this doc ("ok go", same day).
**Game id:** `codequest` (the Lab view of `docs/plans/2026-10-04-lab-of-curiosity/`).
**Builds on:** every decision of `2026-10-04-lab-of-curiosity/design.md` and `2026-10-04-lab-states/design.md` stays in force, except where this doc says it amends one. Amended: Phase 1 **D3** (code-drawn art at a whole-number scale) is kept, but the room is no longer a fixed 320×180 picture (D1 below). The Phase 1 screen sketch is replaced by D1–D3 below.

## Why

On a real tablet the Lab looks small and flat. The room is a fixed 320×180 picture at a whole-number scale, so a 1280×800 tablet at 1× shows it at 3× (960×540) with about 150 CSS px of black on each side. The four workbench tools are 26 px objects in a crowded bottom row, and nothing says what they do. Once an ingredient is lifted, nothing shows where it can go. Ingredients are pictures in jars, not things. Papa wants the Lab to feel like a place, the way Pixel Planet does: full screen, obvious, physical, with depth.

## What the brief got wrong (kept here so the slices don't repeat it)

- "Lab canvas width:100%" does not remove the black edges: the canvas already fills its box; the black is the whole-number fit (`fitLab`). D1 fixes the fit.
- There is no `canBeAppliedTo`: every ingredient takes all three state tools (`STATE_TOOL`). The "rare" multi-target case is every lift.
- There is no `applyIngredient()` and no raycasting. The Lab is a 2D canvas with hit rects; the calls are `dropHeld()` and `processWith(step)` in `lab-screen.js`.
- The scene redraws at ~20 fps (48 ms throttle) on purpose, for battery. Physics needs the full frame rate only while something moves (D5).

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Wider room** (Papa). The whole-number scale is picked exactly as today: `device = max(1, floor(min(canvasW / 320, canvasH / 180)))`, so the 320×180 core always fits. The room then takes the space left: `W = min(floor(canvasW / device), 480)`, `H = min(floor(canvasH / device), 240)`, centred; the leftover is under one logical pixel. Beyond the caps the margin is drawn as more wall and bench texture, never black. The layout becomes `labLayout(W, H)` with three anchored groups — **left** (plants, owl, books, Journal, bag) pinned left; **centre** (window, lantern, cauldron, burner, tools) centred at `floor((W − 320) / 2)`; **right** (shelf + jars, cat, scroll) pinned right at `W − 320` — all bottom-anchored (`dy = H − 180`); extra height becomes more wall and a ceiling beam above. The scene loses its border, radius and gap: it runs edge to edge between the host bar and the dock. The dock (slots, step trail, ↶ ✕, Brew) is unchanged. | Fills the screen with crisp pixels and keeps every Phase 1/2 rule (D3 scale, hits ≥ 48 CSS px). Papa chose it over a room-texture bleed or a fractional stretch. Examples: 1280×800 at 1× → 3×, room 423×210; 1280×800 at 1.5× → 5×, room 381×189; 1024×600 at 1× → 2×, room 480×220. |
| D2 | **Tools 1.5× and a new bench** (Papa). Mortar, spoon and frost plate are redrawn in code at about 39×39 logical px (drawn bigger, not upscaled, so the pixel grid stays clean) and move up onto the bench either side of the cauldron: mortar left, spoon and frost plate right. The burner keeps its width and gets a bigger front dial (24×12); its hit covers the flames and dial. The bag and the scroll keep the bottom row. Hit rects are padded where the room allows. The tested rule holds at every room size: no two hits overlap, each ≥ 24 logical px (48 CSS px at the smallest 2× fit) on its short side. | Big, obvious targets for 6-year-old fingers. The bottom row was 26 px tall with no room to grow. |
| D3 | **Action names below the tools** (Papa). A label plate sits under each tool, always visible: mortar **Grind 研磨**, burner **Heat 加熱**, spoon **Stir 攪拌**, frost plate **Cool 冷卻** — the existing `ALCHEMY_LABELS`, so the plate reads the same word as the step trail in the dock. While an ingredient is lifted, the cauldron gets its own plate **Drop in 放進去**. One language at a time (Phase 1 D12), from the kid's Code Quest setting; every string ships EN + 繁體中文. Plates are DOM chips placed from the hit rects (crisp text, like the "New page!" tag), at least 14 px text, with an icon (🔨 🔥 🥄 ❄️ / ⬇). They take no pointer events; each tool's hit rect extends down over its plate, so tapping the word works. The owl still names the changed form ("Frozen Red Mushroom!"). | Reading the action without guessing from art. Using the step names keeps one word per tool everywhere; Crushed / Heated / Frozen stay the names of the *result*. |
| D4 | **Bobbing arrows** (Papa). **Idle:** cauldron, mortar, burner and frost plate carry a soft glow (alpha 0.3) that breathes on a 2.4 s cycle; with reduced motion it is steady. **Lifted:** those four glow at 0.7 and a pixel ▼ bobs above each one. The spoon is not marked (it never changes an ingredient). **Dragging:** the target under the finger gets a pulsing 1-px ring and its plate brightens; a release within 12 logical px of a target's rect counts as a drop on it (nearest wins). On desktop the cursor turns to a pointer over hits; nothing depends on hover. | Shows "where can this go" with no lines across the room. Forgiving drops for small fingers. |
| D5 | **Gravity, then home** (Papa). Only a drag let go on no target falls. The ingredient keeps its release velocity (last ~80 ms of pointer samples, capped at 400 logical px/s), falls with **g = 500** logical px/s², fall speed capped at **200**, lands on the bench (ground = the lower of the release height and the bench top), slides with frame-rate-independent friction (`v *= 0.92 ** (dt * 60)`), bounces softly off the room sides (restitution 0.4). A dithered shadow on the bench sits under it: small and faint while it is high or being dragged, full when it rests. After ~0.6 s at rest it arcs back to its jar or bag slot (500 ms) with a sparkle. Its changed form, if any, is lost, as today. At most 15 bodies at once; past that the oldest goes home at once. A second tap on a lifted ingredient still puts it down instantly with no fall. Physics lives in a pure `lab-physics.js` (`stepBodies(bodies, dt, world)`). The scene runs at full rAF while a body moves or a drag is active and drops back to the 48 ms throttle when idle. Reduced motion: the body fades and reappears at home. | Physical feel with no new concept: jars are infinite (Phase 1 D5), so loose items that stayed would only pile up. Papa chose this over loose pickable items. |
| D6 | **Depth: a 3D-feeling room, the Pixel Planet way** (Papa: "feel 3D like the planet"). Still code-drawn pixel art on a 2D canvas, palette only — the planet's own technique (Pixel Planet D5 "fake 3D depth"), not WebGL. **Perspective:** back wall, bench top drawn as a receding plane (plank lines converge toward a vanishing point under the window), a bench front edge, and side-wall slivers at the room's left and right edges that show the room has sides. **Light:** three sources — cauldron fire (warm, flickers), window moonlight (cool, from upper centre), lantern (warm, upper left). Surfaces take a 3-band ramp (lit / base / shadow) within the palette, blended through a Bayer 4×4 ordered dither, as on the planet. **Volume:** round props are shaded per pixel from a surface normal — the cauldron as a sphere with a rim light from the fire, jars as cylinders with a vertical glint, the mortar as a bowl. **Shadows:** every object on the bench has a dithered contact shadow; the shelf casts a soft shadow on the wall. **Atmosphere:** a dithered moonlight shaft from the window with drifting dust motes. **Parallax:** the non-interactive back layer (wall, window, lantern, plants) shifts up to ±4 logical px toward a held drag and eases back, plus a slow ±1 px idle sway; everything you can tap stays put, so hits never move. Reduced motion: no parallax, no sway, no flicker. The static back layer is drawn once per room size into an offscreen canvas and blitted (Android 8 budget). | Matches what makes the planet feel 3D (banded light, dither, rim, depth layers) while keeping the Lab's crisp pixels, its tests and its hit model. A real Three.js lab was considered and left out of this plan (see below). |
| D7 | **Unchanged:** resolver, rules, Journal, `brewLab`, effect meanings (`lab-fx.js` gets anchors, not new behaviour), no stars, offline, the dock, hit ids and the `snapshot()` shape (`hits` keep their ids; their numbers change). `model.js`, `interpreter.js`, `parser.js`, `levels.js`, `run.js`, `loot.js`, `progression.js`, `alchemy-code.js` do not change. | Scope: this plan is presentation and input only. |

## Not in this plan

- **A real WebGL / Three.js lab.** Considered for D6 and left out: it would throw away the code-drawn pixel art (Phase 1 D3), every effect in `lab-fx.js`, the hit-rect model and its tests, and it would put the Lab on the Android 8 WebGL1 fallback path. The planet that Papa points to is itself fake 3D on a 2D canvas. If Papa still wants true 3D after seeing D6, it gets its own brainstorm.
- Loose ingredients that stay on the bench; catching a falling ingredient; physics for anything but a missed drop; a moving camera over the interactive layer; new ingredients, rules or states; sound changes.
